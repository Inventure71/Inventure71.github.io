Dream2Detect

A computer vision study testing whether models trained on generated package images can assess damage in real photographs.

Damage scale

Ten bands, from intact to destroyed

Building the training set

Images were generated from thirteen fields covering damage, box shape, tape, background, camera angle, and lighting. Each candidate was reviewed before inclusion in the training set.

  • In an 800-image review pass, 623 kept their generated labels and 177 were relabeled by hand.
  • A second generation round deliberately targeted the real-world cases earlier models kept getting wrong.

Model comparison

Custom CNNs and pretrained backbones

Eight custom CNN versions

I started with custom CNNs and no pretrained weights, then rebuilt them across eight versions. Each one answered a single question — cleaner labels, higher resolution, stricter splits, ordinal losses — and on identical data the real-photo error fell from 2.66 down to 2.11 bands, landing within one band of correct about 39% of the time.

  • V1 existed just to prove the synthetic labels were learnable at all.
  • Longer training and a GroupNorm variant reduced performance.

Pretrained ConvNeXt ensemble

The strongest models paired a ConvNeXt backbone with an Earth Mover's Distance loss — one that punishes a guess three bands off far more than a near miss — blended 60/40 with a SAM-cropped variant. On the same generated dataset, within-one-band accuracy on real photographs increased from 39% to 64%.

  • Exact-band accuracy was 21% on the ten-band test set.
  • Averaging the two views beat either model on its own.

Evaluation design

Splits separated prompt families to reduce leakage. Results on generated and real photographs were reported separately, as were the custom CNN and pretrained model experiments.

  • On synthetic holdout the from-scratch models cleared roughly twice random chance.
  • Performance on real photographs remained the main limitation.

What the experiment showed

The strongest model reached 64% within one severity band on 385 real photographs. Transfer from generated images remained a limitation: exact-band accuracy was 21%, and the experiment does not establish how performance would scale with more training data.
