Budget Buddy

A hackathon project: a budgeting assistant you actually talk to. Instead of yet another dashboard of charts, Budget Buddy is a conversation — it looks at your spending and nudges you toward your goals, minus the guilt trip.

Python LLM Hackathon
Role Hackathon team
Timeline Hackathon build
Stack Python · LLM
Status Weekend prototype

The short version

Budget Buddy pairs your spending with a conversational AI so budgeting feels like talking to a level-headed friend — one who nudges you toward paying down debt and saving without making you feel bad about the latte.

  • Spending breakdowns that follow the conversation, not a static dashboard.
  • Coaches toward your goals by proposing trade-offs instead of shame.

What we built

The pieces behind the conversation

Sees your spending

Pulls your transactions into one view and tidies up messy merchant names, so the picture it talks about is actually honest.

  • Groups spending into categories you can argue with.
  • Flags the weeks that don't look like the others.

Remembers the conversation

Holds onto what you committed to and what you were worried about, so it doesn't turn around and contradict itself next time.

  • Ties chat moments back to what actually happened in your budget.
  • Checks past promises before handing you new advice.

Keeps it private

Sensitive details get masked before anything is sent to the model — a hackathon-scale take on "don't leak the user's finances."

  • The raw numbers stay local; only what's needed goes out.
  • Built fast, but built to not be creepy.

It handles the awkward money talk

The whole idea was a money coach that doesn't lecture. When your goals and your cash flow disagree, it doesn't shame you — it lays out the trade-offs and finds room for the treat once the numbers can actually take it. For a weekend build, that "talk to it like a person" feeling was the part worth chasing.

Flow

How a chat plays out

01

Check in

You tell it what's on your mind — a big purchase, a rough month, a goal — in plain language.

02

Pull the picture

It pulls together the spending that's actually relevant to what you asked, not a wall of every transaction.

03

Talk it through

It suggests a tweak — a budget shift, a debt move, a savings nudge — and explains why, so you can push back.

04

Follow up

Whatever you agree to sticks around, so the next conversation starts where the last one left off.
