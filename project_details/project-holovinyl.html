HoloVinyl

My attempt at reinventing the vinyl record: point the camera at a physical object and the music mapped to it starts playing. Put it down and it stops. No screens to tap, no phone — just objects and a pinch of your fingers.

Computer Vision Gesture UI Python
Role Solo Builder · CV Engineer
Timeline 2025
Stack Python · YOLO11 · MediaPipe
Status Working prototype

The short version

Show an object to the webcam; a custom YOLO model recognizes it and plays the Spotify track, playlist, or album I've mapped to it. A pinch of the fingers acts as a click, so the whole thing is controllable in mid-air.

  • Object detection, hand tracking, and the UI all run together in one live loop, capped at 30 FPS.
  • Teach it a new object from just 5 photos — background removal turns them into training data, no manual labeling.

The core idea

Objects, not buttons

  • Objects as records

    Each object maps to a Spotify source. Show it and its music joins the mix; take it away and it fades out. Several objects can play at once.

  • Pinch to click

    Hand tracking watches your fingers — when thumb and index snap together, that's a click on whatever on-screen button you're pointing at.

  • Teach it in seconds

    Name a new object, snap five photos, hit train. It auto-labels them with background removal and fine-tunes YOLO — no dataset wrangling.

Under the hood

Vision, hands, and a little local AI

Object recognition (YOLO11)

A fine-tuned YOLO11-nano model reads the objects on the surface, so playback follows what's physically in front of the camera.

  • A class only counts as "active" after a few steady frames, so music doesn't flicker.
  • Detections decay when the object leaves, fading its track out.

Hands as a mouse

MediaPipe hand landmarks turn a pinch into a cursor click on buttons you've drawn over the camera view.

  • An ArUco marker board keeps camera space and UI space aligned.
  • The pinch only fires when your fingers close from far to near — no accidental clicks.

A song from anything

Show it something it has never seen and local models can still turn the object into a music query; only Spotify playback needs the network.

  • A local moondream model describes the object in view.
  • deepseek turns that into a genre/mood query, and Spotify plays the match.

The record player, in reverse

The whole idea is nostalgia flipped inside out: instead of lowering a needle onto vinyl, you set an object on the table and its music comes to life. The AI inference stays local on small models, while Spotify connectivity handles the actual playback.

Flow

How a session plays out

01

Calibrate

Four ArUco markers define the surface, and the camera feed is warped into a flat, top-down view of the table.

02

Detect

YOLO reads the objects on the board while MediaPipe tracks your hand — both feeding into one shared loop.

03

React

A steady object starts its music; a pinch on a drawn button plays, pauses, or skips.

04

Play

Commands fire off to Spotify, which handles the actual playback across whatever objects are currently on the table.
