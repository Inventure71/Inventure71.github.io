ClipClop

Copy something on my Mac, and a second later it's on my phone. ClipClop is a local-first clipboard bridge between macOS and Android — no cloud, no account, just two devices talking over your own Wi-Fi.

Python Android Local-first
Role Solo Builder · Systems Designer
Timeline 2024
Stack Python · Java · AES-256-GCM
Status Working prototype

The short version

ClipClop keeps the clipboard in sync between my Mac and my Android phone, entirely over the local network. Copy a link, a paragraph, or a screenshot on one device and it lands on the other — encrypted end to end, never touching a server.

  • The Mac pushes changes the instant they happen — it watches a near-free change counter, not the clipboard's contents.
  • Pairing is a single QR scan, and reconnection is automatic even when my phone's IP changes.
Desktop Repository Android Companion

Where it earns its keep

The moments I built it for

  • Links & snippets

    Copy a URL or a block of code on my laptop and paste it on my phone a second later — no AirDrop dance, no emailing things to myself.

  • Screenshots too

    Images ride the same pipe as text, so a screenshot copied on one device is already waiting in the clipboard on the other.

  • Café Wi-Fi, no worries

    Every message is AES-256-GCM encrypted with a key that only ever travels by QR code, so the traffic stays opaque even on a shared network.

Under the hood

Three pieces, talking over your LAN

A quiet Mac menu-bar app

No Dock icon — it lives in the menu bar. Instead of reading the clipboard on a timer, it watches NSPasteboard's change counter and only does real work when something actually changes.

  • Shows connection status and the last device it paired with.
  • Pairing generates a fresh 256-bit key and hands it over as a QR code.

An Android foreground service

A foreground service keeps the TCP connection alive and writes incoming clips straight to the system clipboard.

  • Mac → Android is automatic; Android → Mac is one deliberate tap.
  • It rediscovers the paired Mac on its own after any network change.

Encryption & discovery

Every message is AES-256-GCM with a fresh nonce, and the two devices find each other again through a three-tier fallback.

  • Bonjour/mDNS first, a UDP beacon as backup, manual IP as a last resort.
  • Reconnects match the Mac's stable device ID — never a stale IP.

No cloud. No account. Just your two devices.

Nothing ClipClop moves ever leaves your local network. There's no server to trust, no sync account to create, and the encryption key is exchanged by scanning a QR code — it never crosses the wire. It's the clipboard sync I always wished existed: private because of how it's built, not because of a privacy policy.

Flow

How a clip gets across

01

Pair

Scan a QR code from the Mac's menu bar. It carries the encryption key and the Mac's identity — that's the entire setup.

02

Copy

Copy anything on the Mac. It notices the change instantly and wraps the payload in AES-256-GCM.

03

Land

The clip travels over an encrypted TCP connection on your LAN and drops straight into the other device's clipboard.

04

Reconnect

Switch networks or let your phone sleep — ClipClop finds the Mac again by its device ID and picks up right where it left off.
