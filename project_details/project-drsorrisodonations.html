DrSorrisoDonations

Every donation to the Fondazione Dottor Sorriso arrives as a cryptic bank-transfer memo, with the donor's name buried somewhere in the noise. This bot reads those lines, figures out who actually gave, and files each donation into the charity's platform automatically — the tedious job no volunteer should have to do by hand.

Role Solo builder
Timeline 2024 – 2025
Stack Python · Selenium · GPT-4o-mini

The short version

It logs into the charity's donation platform, reads each incoming transfer's raw description, pulls the donor out of the noise, and enters the donation — matching it to a campaign code or an existing contact, creating one when needed.

  • A multi-call LLM consensus — it asks GPT-4o-mini a few times at different temperatures, then votes on the donor's name.
  • A hand-built Italian name dictionary — 200k+ first names, 800k+ surnames — tells real donors apart from bank codes.

What it actually does

From bank noise to a filed donation

  • Two kinds of donation

    Some transfers carry a campaign code buried in the text; others just have a name. The bot detects which, and routes each down the right path.

  • Names out of noise

    It strips transaction boilerplate, dedups repeated tokens, and cross-checks against a dictionary of Italian names to pull the real donor out.

  • It remembers

    Every parsed donation is cached, so recurring donors are recognized instantly instead of re-running the language model.

Under the hood

Reading, naming, filing

Reading the memo

Italian SEPA transfers are full of noise — TRN codes, "EROGAZIONE LIBERALE", bank BIC codes. A hand-built blacklist and regex strip it back to the essentials.

  • Four fallback regex patterns pull out campaign and donor codes.
  • Whatever's left goes to the language model.

Naming the donor

GPT-4o-mini identifies the first and last names, with domain rules baked in — names are Italian, and never two surnames in a row.

  • A few calls at different temperatures, then a consensus vote.
  • A second pass double-checks the result before it's trusted.

Filing it away

Selenium drives the real donation platform — selecting campaigns, matching contacts, creating new ones, and confirming each entry.

  • Handles error popups, alerts, and a self-migrating local cache.
  • Skips any donor it has already parsed before.

So a volunteer doesn't hand-copy hundreds of donors

This is the least glamorous problem I've ever solved, and one of my favorites. Behind every garbled bank line is a real person — someone who sent a small gift to a foundation that brings joy to hospitalized children. The bot's whole job is to read past the "BONIFICO SEPA … TRN …" and find that person, so the people running the charity can spend their time on the kids instead of on spreadsheets.
