VictorIA

A robot arm that plays Connect 4 against you — on a real board. A webcam reads the grid, a minimax AI picks the move, and the arm drops the disc. No screen, no touch input, just a machine that reaches over and beats you at a physical game.

The short version

VictorIA turns a webcam and a robot arm into a Connect 4 opponent. It calibrates the board from a few clicks, reads every move in real time, thinks with minimax, and physically places its own disc.

  • SAM 2 segments the board once; a lightweight classifier reads each cell every frame.
  • Vision and AI run on my Mac; the arm runs on a separate ROS machine, talking over HTTP.

Three things had to work

See the board, think, then move

  • See the board

    Click a few points, SAM 2 masks the board, and a perspective warp flattens the webcam feed into a clean, top-down grid.

  • Read every move

    A classifier tags each of the 42 cells as empty, mine, or yours — and only re-checks the cells where a new disc could actually have landed.

  • Pick and place

    Minimax with alpha-beta pruning chooses the column, and the Interbotix arm lifts a disc from its tower and drops it in.

Under the hood

Two vision models and two machines

Finding the board (SAM 2)

Segment Anything 2 localizes the board once from a few clicked points; line-fitting the mask edges gives four corners for the warp.

  • No jig or fixed mount — it adapts to wherever the camera sits.
  • I confirm the calibration before the game starts.

Reading the cells

A Teachable Machine classifier reads each 224×224 cell as green, red, or empty, then assembles a 6×7 matrix.

  • Only cells reachable by the next drop get re-classified each frame.
  • Single-core and multithreaded paths both exist.

Two machines, one game

Vision and AI run on the Mac; the physical arm runs on a separate ROS box, coordinated over a single HTTP move endpoint.

  • Minimax searches five plies deep with alpha-beta pruning.
  • The arm knows column spacing, a disc "tower", and safe travel poses.

It's a see-think-act loop in a robot costume

Strip away the arm and VictorIA is one tight loop: see the board, decide the move, move, repeat. The fun part was getting a heavy segmentation model and a tiny fast classifier to share that loop — one for the one-time setup, one for every single frame — so the whole thing stays responsive while a real motor swings over a real board.

Flow

How one turn plays out

01

Calibrate

Click the board's corners; SAM 2 masks it and a perspective warp gives a clean top-down view.

02

Read

Each frame, the classifier reads the 6×7 grid and diffs it against the last to spot the move you just made.

03

Decide

Minimax with alpha-beta pruning searches five moves ahead and settles on a column.

04

Move

That column is POSTed to the arm's ROS machine, which picks up a disc and drops it into place.
