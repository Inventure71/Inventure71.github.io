macOS automation utility

TypeCraft

Give TypeCraft a block of text and it types into whichever Mac app you choose with configurable rhythm, thinking pauses, bursts, and believable typos that it notices and corrects.

Choose the destination after pressing Start

The unusual part is the handoff: TypeCraft waits for three global clicks before emitting a key. That gives you time to leave its panel, focus the real destination, and confirm where the text should land.

  • The third click commits the destination; pause and resume repeat the same safety step.
  • System-wide CGEvent keystrokes work across focused applications after explicit Accessibility permission.

One controlled sequence

Prepare, focus, type

01

Shape the rhythm

Paste the text, choose a built-in or saved custom preset, then tune speed variation, punctuation pauses, thinking time, typo frequency, and burst behavior.

02

Hand off focus

Press Start and click three times. The floating panel reports the countdown while you select the destination application.

03

Emit and correct

The service posts characters at a changing cadence, selects nearby QWERTY keys for simulated mistakes, waits, backspaces, and types the intended character.

04

Stay in control

Progress, pause, resume, and stop remain available from the floating panel and menu-bar app. App Nap is disabled only while TypeCraft is active or waiting.

Automation that makes focus consequential

TypeCraft is a released utility, not a typing trainer: it has no drills or analytics. It requires Accessibility permission and types wherever focus sits after click three, which is why the explicit countdown and always-available stop controls are part of the core design.
